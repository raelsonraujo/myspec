## Gates

| Reported value | Threshold | Applies to | Enforcement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Highest cyclomatic complexity among methods | `≤ 15` | Both | Attention |
| Methods above 15 | `= 0`, except methods tolerated below | Both | Attention |

These targets match the default boundary of the repository-pinned ReportGenerator, which presents complexity above 15 as elevated. Treat 15 as the upper boundary of the normal range, not as a value methods should approach, and read this gate together with the CRAP Score gate:

- a method with complexity from 16 through 25 is tolerated when its CRAP score is 30 or lower, its relevant decision paths have meaningful automated coverage, and its branching represents one cohesive responsibility;
- a method with complexity above 25 remains an improvement candidate regardless of its CRAP score;
- complexity does not improve merely because branches moved into arbitrary helper methods; extracted methods must have cohesive, behavior-oriented responsibilities.

Prefer naturally lower complexity when it follows from a clear, cohesive design, but never at the expense of readability, cohesion, or useful domain structure.

## Decisions and Tradeoffs

- Use Coverlet's Cobertura complexity instead of a separate complexity tool: one test run yields coverage, cyclomatic complexity, and CRAP score for the same code.
- Pin ReportGenerator as a repository-local tool: developers and CI use the same aggregation behavior without a global installation.
- Exclude migrations and generated files: their generated branching and volume would distort developer-controlled metrics.
- Use `Attention` gates: a method outside the normal range is an improvement signal rather than a blocked inspection until the team explicitly adopts `Blocking` gates.
